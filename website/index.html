Why existing solutions fail

  • Big Tech proprietary systems: controlled by US corporations; antitrust exposure, rejected by other blocs.
  • Nation-state standards: seen as geopolitical tools, rejected abroad.
  • Paper-only guidelines: no code, no cryptography, no way to actually verify.
  • Human-only identity protocols: do not verify AI agents or AI-generated content.

The consequence of inaction

  • Fragmentation. Every nation builds its own → 10+ incompatible systems → global trade friction.
  • Geopolitical capture. One bloc's standard dominates → a digital Iron Curtain.
  • Unverifiable AI. Regulations exist but cannot be enforced → public trust erodes.

The proposal: four pillars

Four verifiable layers working together as one system. No existing framework covers all four.

1

AI & Human Identity

Who or what is acting?

  • Decentralized Identifiers (DID) extended for AI agents: did:cyber:agent
  • Human identity via W3C Verifiable Credentials
  • AI identity: cryptographic key bound to model, version, and operator

You can distinguish a human from an AI, and know which AI, instantly.

2

Provenance & Output Certification

Where did this come from?

  • Every AI response, file, or transaction carries an immutable cryptographic seal
  • Seal includes model ID, timestamp, configuration hash, and operator signature
  • Zero-knowledge option: prove origin without revealing trade secrets

You can trust what you see, and trace it to its source, permanently.

3

Safety & Risk Compliance

Does this meet legal requirements?

  • Compliance metadata maps directly to EU AI Act risk categories
  • NIST AI RMF alignment fields included
  • ISO/IEC 42001 audit-ready attestation

One proof, recognized by multiple regulatory frameworks simultaneously.

4

Cross-Border Verification

Is this proof valid everywhere?

  • Neutral format: not tied to any nation's signature scheme
  • Built for UN ECOSOC, EU, WAICO, and Global South adoption
  • Open validation: anyone can verify the seal, no proprietary software

One standard, recognized in every jurisdiction, without geopolitical restriction.

Design principles

Five commitments that make the Standard trustworthy by construction.

Neutral

No single entity controls the protocol. Governance is multi-stakeholder.

Open

Specification under CC BY 4.0; reference code under Apache 2.0. Free to read, use, and implement.

Interoperable

Built on existing standards (W3C, ISO, NIST), not competing with them.

Privacy-first

Zero-knowledge proofs enable compliance without exposing confidential data.

Permissionless

No gatekeepers. Any developer can implement it; any verifier can validate it.

Technical foundation

We are not inventing new cryptography. We are unifying proven standards into one coherent framework. The building blocks are ready. Only coordination is needed, which we provide.
